1. WHAT IS THIS PROJECT? (IN 3 SIMPLE SENTENCES)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This project is a web-based Queueing Simulator for a Petrol Pump station.
It calculates how long cars have to wait in line, how long they spend at the pump,
and how busy the petrol pumps are. It uses real numbers recorded from 300 cars at
a real petrol station and tests 5 different queueing formulas.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. HOW THE SOFTWARE WORKS (SIMPLE ARCHITECTURE)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The software has 3 parts:

1. FRONTEND (User Interface):
   - index.html & style.css: A dark, modern dashboard inspired by a night-time
     petrol station.
   - app.js: Reads your inputs (arrival time, service time, number of pumps) and
     sends them to the backend server.

2. BACKEND (Express Server):
   - server.js: A lightweight Node.js server. When you click "Calculate", it
     receives your numbers, runs the math, and sends back the results as JSON.

3. CALCULATION ENGINE (The Math):
   - queueModels.js: Contains the queueing math equations.
   - petrolData.js: Contains the real dataset numbers measured at the petrol pump.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. THE REAL DATASET USED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
We observed 300 vehicles over 6 days (50 vehicles per day):

- Average Arrival Time : 40.15 seconds between cars
- Average Service Time : 39.94 seconds to fuel a car
- Standard Deviation   : ~23.5 seconds (capturing variations: some cars buy more
                         petrol, some drivers pay faster)

IMPORTANT POINT TO TELL YOUR TEACHER:
"Cars arrive every 40.15 seconds, but fueling takes 39.94 seconds.
With only 1 pump, utilization is 99.5%! That means 1 pump is almost overloaded
and the line becomes huge. Adding a 2nd pump cuts the load in half (~50%) and
eliminates the long queue."

4. KEY SYMBOLS YOU MUST KNOW (TEACHER WILL ASK THESE!)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Symbol   What it Means                  In Plain English
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
lambda   Arrival Rate                   How many cars arrive per minute/hour
mu       Service Rate                   How many cars one pump can fuel per min
c        Number of Pumps                How many pumps are working (1, 2, 3...)
rho      Pump Utilization               How busy the pump is (must be < 100%)
Lq       Queue Length                   Average number of cars WAITING in line
L        System Length                  Average number of cars in the whole station
Wq       Waiting Time in Queue          How many minutes a driver waits in line
W        Total Time in System           Waiting time + Fueling time
P0       Empty Probability              Chance the petrol station has zero cars

STABILITY RULE:
"rho must be LESS than 1 (rho < 1). If rho >= 1, cars arrive faster than pumps
can fuel them, and the queue grows infinitely large."

LITTLE'S LAW:
- L  = lambda * W   (Total cars = Arrival rate * Total time)
- Lq = lambda * Wq  (Cars waiting = Arrival rate * Waiting time)
- W  = Wq + (1 / mu) (Total time = Waiting time + Fueling time)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. THE 5 QUEUE MODELS (EASY EXPLANATION)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

MODEL 1: M/M/1 (Single Pump, Simple Random Times)
- Meaning: 1 pump. Arrivals and service times follow an exponential distribution.
- Formula:
    Lq = rho^2 / (1 - rho)
- When to use: When arrival and service are purely random with only 1 pump.

MODEL 2: M/G/1 (Single Pump, Any Service Variation)
- Meaning: 1 pump. Arrivals are random, but service has a custom standard deviation.
- Formula (Pollaczek-Khinchine):
    Lq = (lambda^2 * sigma^2 + rho^2) / [ 2 * (1 - rho) ]
- Key Concept: Shows that higher variance (sigma) causes LONGER waiting lines.

MODEL 3: G/G/1 (Single Pump, Real-World Data)
- Meaning: 1 pump. Neither arrival nor service is purely exponential; both have
  measured averages and standard deviations.
- Formula (Sakasegawa Approximation):
    Wq = [ rho / (1 - rho) ] * [ (Ca^2 + Cs^2) / 2 ] * (1 / mu)

MODEL 4: M/M/c (Multiple Pumps, e.g., 2 or more pumps)
- Meaning: 'c' identical pumps sharing one common line of cars.
- Formula (Erlang-C):
    Calculates P(Wait) = chance that all pumps are busy when a car arrives.
    Lq = [ P(Wait) * rho ] / (1 - rho)
- Key Concept: Shows how opening more pumps dramatically drops customer waiting time.

MODEL 5: G/G/c (Multiple Pumps, Real-World Data)
- Meaning: Multiple pumps where arrival and service both have standard deviations.
- Formula (Sakasegawa Multi-Server):
    Wq = [ rho^(sqrt(2(c+1)) - 1) / (c * (1 - rho)) ] * [ (Ca^2 + Cs^2) / 2 ] * (1 / mu)

6. QUICK VIVA / TEACHER QUESTIONS (JUST MEMORIZE THESE 5!)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q1: What does Kendall's Notation (e.g., M/M/c) stand for?
Answer:
"The 1st letter is Arrival type (M = Markovian/Poisson, G = General).
The 2nd letter is Service type (M = Exponential, G = General).
The 3rd letter 'c' is the number of pumps."

Q2: What is 'rho' (Utilization) and why must it be under 1?
Answer:
"rho is how busy the pumps are: rho = lambda / (c * mu). It must be less than 1.
If rho >= 1, cars enter faster than pumps can serve them, meaning the queue
never ends and the system is unstable."

Q3: What is Little's Law?
Answer:
"Little's Law connects queue length and waiting time: L = lambda * W and
Lq = lambda * Wq. Total time W is simply waiting time Wq plus service time."

Q4: Why does variance (standard deviation) matter in queueing?
Answer:
"The Pollaczek-Khinchine formula proves that even if average service time is
the same, higher variation (inconsistent fueling times) makes lines much longer."

Q5: What was your main finding from the real petrol pump data?
Answer:
"With 1 pump, arrival time (40.15s) and service time (39.94s) are nearly equal,
giving 99.5% utilization, causing extreme delays. Having 2 pumps reduces
utilization to ~50% and eliminates long queues."
